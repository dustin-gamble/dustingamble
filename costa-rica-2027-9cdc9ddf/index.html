The short version

What this study concludes

Five findings drove everything below. If you read nothing else, read these.

1. Costco is the wrong product for this trip

Costco Travel's entire Costa Rica catalog is Guanacaste — Papagayo and Reserva Conchal beach resorts, flying into Liberia. There is not a single jungle property on offer. It's a good deal on a different vacation. See the math →

2. Fly into San José, not Liberia

Both have LAX nonstops. But LIR serves dry tropical forest and beach resorts on the Pacific northwest — the opposite of jungle. SJO puts you 3 hours from Arenal and one puddle-jumper from the Osa.

3. Target mid-July for the veranillo

The "little summer" is a genuine mid-rainy-season dry spell on the Pacific side and in the Central Valley. Same lush jungle and low-season pricing, meaningfully less rain. Rainfall chart →

4. Two bases is exactly right for 10 days

Eight nights in country. Four and four. Every additional base costs you roughly half a day of transit and a night of unpacking, which is a bad trade at this length.

5. Recommended: Osa Peninsula + Arenal

You said "mostly jungle." The Osa is the only honest answer — Corcovado holds roughly 2.5% of global biodiversity. The kids are exactly old enough for the full Sirena hike. Estimated — for four at mixed tier, which is the priciest of the three.

The runner-up depends on what you want

Manuel Antonio (—) for the lowest-friction version, real beach days, and monkeys around the pool most afternoons. Tortuguero (—) if peak green-turtle nesting appeals more than Corcovado — July/August is the only window.

Value check

The Costco question

You asked how the packaged deals look. Short answer: the pricing is real, but the geography is wrong.

Every Costa Rica package Costco sells is in Guanacaste. Their destination page lists exactly one region under "Cities in Costa Rica," and it's Guanacaste. The seven featured deals are Nekajui (Ritz-Carlton Reserve), Westin Reserva Conchal, Planet Hollywood, JW Marriott Guanacaste, W Costa Rica, Andaz Peninsula Papagayo, and Secrets Papagayo — all Pacific-northwest beach resorts, all served by Liberia.

The honest trade. Costco's Guanacaste packages are genuinely cheaper — plausibly half the cost of the Osa option — and much simpler to book. If you want packaged convenience and jungle

The middle path is a Costa Rica specialist operator rather than a warehouse-club resort bundler. They price multi-base jungle itineraries as one number with transfers included, typically at a 10–20% premium over booking direct — often less once you count the domestic flights and transfers they negotiate.

Timing

Go mid-July

Costa Rican summer is green season. That's an advantage, not a problem — but where in summer matters a great deal.

Pacific / Central Valley rainfall pattern

Relative monthly rainfall, Pacific slope. Highlighted bars show the veranillo dip. Late Sept–Oct is the genuine washout.

What a green-season day looks like

Clear, hot morning. Clouds build around 1–2pm. A hard downpour late afternoon. Clearing by evening. You plan activities for mornings. It is not all-day drizzle.

Why green season is actually better here

  • Jungle at maximum lushness; waterfalls running hard
  • Wildlife active and breeding
  • Rates 20–40% below dry season
  • Parks close to empty — the Corcovado difference is dramatic

Regional caveat that shapes the options. The veranillo barely reaches the Osa Peninsula (still sunny mornings, heavy afternoon rain) and does not reach the Caribbean at all — July is one of Tortuguero's wetter months. Guanacaste, where Costco sells, is the driest corner of the country in July. That's not a coincidence; it's why the resorts are there.

Target dates: July 8–17 or July 15–24, 2027. Avoid late September and October, when several lodges close outright.

Logistics

Getting there

🚗 Central Coast → LAX

~190–200 mi via US-101. 3.5 hrs clean, 4.5–5 hrs realistic. For a late-night departure, leave by 2–3pm.

Parking: LAX Economy ~$20/day. Off-airport (WallyPark, The Parking Spot) ~$12–16/day. Price a Park-Sleep-Fly hotel for the return so you're not driving home exhausted.

✈️ LAX → SJO nonstop

Alaska, United, and Delta all fly it nonstop; Southwest sells it too. ~6h05m, roughly 9–10 departures weekly.

Departures spread from midday to 23:30. Book the late-evening one — it lands ~6:30–7:00am and buys you a full extra day.

🕐 Time zone

Costa Rica is UTC−6 with no daylight saving, so in July it runs one hour ahead of California. Jet lag is essentially a non-issue — this is the easiest long-haul time zone you'll ever fly to with kids.

Do this week: check all four passport expiration dates. Costa Rica technically requires validity for the length of stay, but airlines routinely enforce a six-month rule. Kids' passports are five-year documents — there's a real chance the kids' expire before mid-2027, and renewal backlogs are not forgiving. This is the single highest-risk item on the whole plan and it costs nothing to check.

Rental car gotcha. Quoted rates online exclude Costa Rica's mandatory liability insurance (SLI/TPL), roughly $15–25/day, added at the counter and not waivable by your credit card. A "$12/day" quote is really $35–45/day. Book direct with Adobe or Vamos, who quote all-in. Get a 4x4 in green season — not optional on secondary roads.

The decision

Base 1 is fixed. Base 2 is the choice.

Arenal / La Fortuna anchors every option — it's the highest-density family adventure region in the country, sits in genuine lowland rainforest under a 5,400-ft volcano, and is close enough to SJO to reach on arrival morning. The real decision is what you pair it with.

A · Osa Peninsula

Drake Bay · 4 nights · deep jungle

Corcovado holds roughly 2.5% of the planet's biodiversity. All four monkey species, tapirs, flocks of scarlet macaws, peccary herds. No road in during rainy season — you arrive by small plane and land on a beach.

  • Best for: the actual assignment
  • Access: 1-hr Sansa flight, ~$160/pp each way
  • Watch out: wettest option, remote, priciest
  • Kid fit: 11 & 12 is the perfect age for Sirena
Est. total, 4 pax—

B · Manuel Antonio

Central Pacific · 4 nights · jungle + beach

Rainforest running straight down to white-sand swimming beaches. Squirrel monkeys, capuchins, and sloths inside the first twenty minutes of trail. The easiest, most proven family week in the country.

  • Best for: lowest friction, real beach days
  • Access: 4.5–5 hr drive from Arenal, all paved
  • Watch out: most developed and touristed
  • Kid fit: excellent — surf lessons, waterfalls
  • 🐒 Only option with monkeys at the pool — see why
Est. total, 4 pax—

C · Tortuguero

Caribbean · 3 nights + Sarapiquí · canal jungle

A road-less village on a sand strip between jungle canals and the sea. July and August are the absolute peak of green turtle nesting — thousands of 300-lb females coming ashore. The only option with a hard seasonal reason to go now.

  • Best for: a once-in-a-lifetime headline
  • Access: boat only — shuttle + 1.5 hr canal ride
  • Watch out: no veranillo here; genuinely wet
  • Kid fit: the turtle tour is unforgettable at 11/12

Special request

🐒 Monkeys around the pool

This is a legitimate way to choose a hotel, and it separates the options more sharply than you'd expect. Short answer: Manuel Antonio delivers this daily and near-reliably. Nowhere else does.

Manuel Antonio Best by far

Three species live on hotel grounds here, not just nearby: squirrel monkeys, white-faced capuchins, and howlers. The hotels are built into the forest strip along the ridge, so the troops move through the properties as part of their daily circuit.

When: squirrel monkey troops typically pass through mid-to-late afternoon, roughly 3–4pm, and again near dusk.

Osa / Drake Bay More monkeys, fewer pools

You will see more monkeys and more species here than anywhere else — all four Costa Rican species including spider monkeys, which Manuel Antonio doesn't have. But you'll see them in the forest on guided hikes, not lounging by a pool. The lodges are small and several don't have big pool decks at all.

Howlers will wake you at 4:30am. That counts for something.

Arenal Weakest

The surprise. Arenal is superb for sloths, coatis, toucans, and birds, but it's the weakest of the four regions for monkeys. Howlers are around and audible; white-faced troops are hit-or-miss and rarely on resort grounds.

Arenal Observatory Lodge is the best bet in the region — howler troops and coatis regularly cross the property.

How this should change the decision. If monkeys-at-the-pool is a top-three priority rather than a nice-to-have, that's a real argument for Option B — and it's the cheapest option too. A middle path worth considering: keep the Osa but swap two Arenal nights for two in Manuel Antonio on the way back to San José. It adds a base, which cuts against the two-base logic, but the drive from Manuel Antonio to SJO is only about three hours.

Rules, and they matter with kids. Never feed monkeys — it's illegal in Costa Rica, it spreads disease in both directions, and it turns troops aggressive toward the next family. Keep balcony doors and windows shut when you're out; capuchins are genuinely skilled at opening bags and raiding rooms. Don't try to touch or hold one, and keep food and drinks off unattended pool tables.

Day by day

Itineraries

All three assume the late-evening LAX departure, eight nights in country, and a Saturday-to-Sunday shape. Switch between them.

Sequencing note for Option A. Put the Osa first, Arenal second. Landing SJO at 7am and connecting straight to the Sansa flight saves a full backtrack day, and it means you're not gambling an international departure on a puddle-jumper at the end of the trip. Options B and C run the other way — Arenal first — because both second bases are reachable by road.
