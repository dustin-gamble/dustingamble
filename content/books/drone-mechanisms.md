## About the book

A companion to [Drone Field Guide](https://gamble.systems/drone-field-guide/) and [Drone Sketchbook](/books/drone-sketchbook/), and a book of parts.

The Sketchbook is whole aircraft. This one is the small moving parts that go into them: hinges, latches, folds and joints, the bits that come off without a tool and the bits that let go before something breaks. About a thousand of them, four to a page. Each one says what it removes, how it works in a sentence or two, the one number that decides whether it holds, how it fails, and one variation to try.

None of it is a set of plans. It is a book to read with a problem in mind, and the point is the idea you have on the way through.

## Inside

The mechanisms are grouped by the job they do, not by what they look like, in twenty-five chapters:

- Comes off without a tool, and only goes on one way.
- Snaps together, folds once, folds every flight.
- Lets go on purpose, and lets go before something breaks.
- Holds with the power off, and shows it is locked.
- Holds the battery, joins two tubes, moves a surface, mounts the motor, carries the camera.
- Done with tape, and no moving parts at all.
- Keeps the shake out, keeps the weather out, fits the hand.
- Borrowed from somewhere else.

Every mechanism is drawn so the trick can be seen at a glance, open and shut, folded and flying, and carries a small score card: how well it holds, how light it is, how quick it is to use, how easy it is to make, and how long it lasts.

## A taste

> Strips of tape laid over and under a gap, so a surface swings both ways with no bevel. A hatch held by two magnets and a tongue. A propeller that folds back when the motor stops. A wing held on with rubber bands, so a cartwheel knocks it askew instead of breaking it. A latch that will not lie flat until its pin is home.
>
> Open it anywhere and take one to the bench.

## Status

In progress. All 1,003 mechanisms are written, across 25 chapters, and the review copy runs about 300 pages. The plates are still being drawn, and each one is checked against a single question before it goes in: would the mechanism work as drawn. It is not on sale yet.

The numbers are first-order estimates with their conditions. Every entry reads *Drawn, not built* until I build one at home and measure it.

It was made the way the other two were. I set the chapters, the format and the bar each entry has to clear. An AI writing assistant drafted the entries from that direction, and I am going through them now: keep, cut or build. An AI illustrator draws the plates, and separate reviewers check each one.
