## About the book

A companion to [Drone Field Guide](https://gamble.systems/drone-field-guide/), and a book of ideas.

Two hundred small unmanned aircraft that could be built, one to a page. Each is drawn and described the same way: what it is, why anyone would build it, what you get and what it costs, the one number that decides whether it flies, and whether anyone has flown one.

None of it is a set of plans. It is a sketchbook. The point is that somewhere around the fortieth page an idea you did not come in with starts to form.

## Inside

The designs are grouped by the constraint that forced the shape, not by what they look like:

- Aircraft that stay up for days on the sun, the wind, a power line or a warm pond.
- Aircraft that need no runway because the runway is a hill, a wave, a tube or a stairwell.
- Wings that fold, roll, inflate or unroll to fit a box.
- Shapes that change in flight.
- Machines made of card, ice, fungus and bamboo.
- Designs built to crash well.
- Aircraft that only work when they are tiny, and aircraft that only close when they are huge.
- Kites, gliders and balloons older than the aeroplane, rebuilt with a controller in place of a pilot.
- A chapter of designs that are buildable, demonstrable, and indistinguishable from a hoax.

## A taste

> A lopsided twin with the motor on one boom and the camera on the other. A sailplane that lands on a hilltop for the night so it never has to carry a night battery. A quad that climbs a stairwell step by step. A wing frozen from water on a glacier and left to melt. A helicopter with one blade. A brick that lifts out of a wall.
>
> Every one of them starts from a constraint nobody wants and ends somewhere nobody expected, and every one of them comes with the number that decides whether it flies.

## Status

In progress. All 222 designs are written, across 24 categories, and the layout proof runs about 260 pages. The plates are still being drawn, so it is not ready to print.

It was made the way Drone Field Guide was made. I set the categories, the designs, and the number that decides each one. An AI writing assistant drafted the pages from that direction, and an AI illustrator drew the plates from my briefs. None of the plates depicts a real aircraft or program.
